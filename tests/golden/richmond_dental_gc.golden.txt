## job_summary
Commercial tenant fit-out converting a 1,600 sq ft former retail space into a dental office in Richmond, VA. Scope triggers a building permit for interior demolition/new partitions and accessibility upgrades, plus separate trade permits for plumbing rough-in (four operatories with vacuum/compressed air and nitrous oxide), electrical panel upgrade and dedicated equipment circuits, and mechanical work for rooftop unit replacement with ductwork rework. Because this is a commercial change of use/tenant improvement, expect plan review and coordinated inspections across building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical disciplines.

## pro_tips

  - Submit the building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits together with one coordinated plan set so plan review comments come back in one cycle instead of four separate ones.

  - Have the RTU submittal, duct layout, and electrical load calc ready before filing — commercial HVAC plan review in Richmond goes faster when the equipment schedule matches the drawings exactly.

  - For dental offices, include a clear equipment schedule for chairs, compressors, vacuum pumps, and X-ray units; that prevents electrical corrections on dedicated circuits and panel sizing.

  - Check your Virginia contractor license classification and expiration date before opening the portal — license issues are a common reason commercial applications get rejected.

  - If the project touches any fire-rated corridor or tenant separation wall, get that detail on the plans up front; otherwise the building reviewer will kick it back for life-safety clarification.

  - health-care licensing paths before pricing.

  - Coordinate architect/MEP, medical-gas verifier, equipment vendor, and health/licensing reviewer before submitting so room names, fixture schedules, ventilation, and equipment utility loads match.

  - verify B vs I-2/ambulatory-care status with the building department if any procedure, sedation, recovery, or self-preservation issue exists.

## common_mistakes

  - Submitting only trade permits and skipping the building permit for the change of use and new partitions — Richmond will hold the job up in plan review

  - Trying to install the larger RTU without matching duct sizing and load calculations to the approved plans

  - Under-sizing the electrical service or panel after adding dedicated dental equipment circuits, which can force a redesign mid-review

  - Treating nitrous oxide piping like a simple fixture tie-in instead of a commercial medical-gas-related plumbing scope that needs clear plan documentation

  - Starting demo before the permit set is approved and posted — inspectors can issue a stop-work order

  - Submitting permit applications without verifying current Virginia contractor license status and trade classification — an expired or mismatched license will stall issuance

  - Leaving medical gas, nitrous/oxygen, alarms, zone valves, and verifier paperwork out of the permit package.

  - Using ordinary office HVAC assumptions instead of confirming ventilation, exhaust, pressure relationships, filtration, and infection-control needs for procedure/sterilization/lab rooms.

  - check-in counters.

## Inspections

  [Building Rough-In / Framing] Inspector checks new partition layout, fireblocking/draftstopping where applicable, rated wall continuity if any tenant separation or corridor rating is involved, door swings/clearances, ADA restroom layout, and that no structural members were altered without approval.

     Timing: After demo and framing are complete, before insulation, drywall, or ceiling closure

  [Plumbing Rough-In] Inspector verifies operatory vacuum and compressed-air piping routing/support, nitrous oxide piping installation and labeling, pipe material compatibility, pressure test results, restroom rough-in locations, trap/venting, and access to shutoffs.

     Timing: Before walls are closed and before fixtures are set

  [Electrical Rough-In / Service] Inspector checks panel upgrade installation, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, dedicated circuits for dental chairs and X-ray equipment, working clearances at the panel, breaker labeling, and any required AFCI/GFCI protection where applicable.

     Timing: Before insulation and drywall; service work may need utility coordination before final inspection

  [Mechanical Rough-In / Equipment Set] Inspector verifies RTU set on approved curb/support, equipment clearances, ductwork sizing and sealing, condensate drainage, outside air/return air arrangement, thermostat/control wiring, and that the larger unit matches approved plans and load calculations.

     Timing: After RTU and duct modifications are installed, before ceiling closure and startup

  [Final Building / Trade Finals] Inspector confirms all finishes are complete, ADA restroom is usable, all fixtures and devices are installed, equipment is operational, labels and access panels are in place, and all trade finals pass before occupancy.

     Timing: After all work is complete and equipment is energized/tested
