## Companion Permits

  - Health Department / Food Establishment Review: Restaurant scopes with food-prep, kitchen, bar, sink, dishwashing, or opening work commonly require health review before opening.

  - Grease Interceptor / FOG Approval: Commercial kitchen plumbing often requires grease interceptor/FOG approval.

  - Accessibility / ADA path-of-travel verification: Commercial tenant improvements commonly trigger accessible route, restroom, door/hardware, counter, parking, or 20% disproportionality review.

## pro_tips

  - Get the Water & Sewer Planning and Engineering sign-off first; that is the Savannah-specific gate that most often delays restaurant TI permits.

  - Bundle the building, plumbing, mechanical, electrical, and fire protection submittals with one coordinated plan set so plan review does not bounce the job between departments.

  - Use a realistic valuation on the permit application — Savannah's building fee is $8 per $1,000, so a clean, accurate valuation avoids correction cycles.

  - If the project is in or near the Historic District, check for Historic District Board review before you submit; that can add time even for interior renovation work.

  - Verify contractor registration and license expiration dates before opening the portal — a lapsed credential is one of the fastest ways to get a commercial permit rejected.

## common_mistakes

  - Submitting the commercial building permit application without the Water & Sewer Planning and Engineering signed approval form — Savannah will not accept the renovation application without it.

  - Trying to pull the whole restaurant build-out under only a building permit — the plumbing, mechanical, electrical, and fire protection scopes still need their own trade permits.

  - Understating project valuation to reduce fees — Savannah uses valuation-based fees and can reprice the permit if the declared value is unrealistic.

  - Submitting permit applications without verifying current contractor license status — an expired or lapsed license will block the application and delay review.

## Inspections

  [Building Rough-In / Framing Inspection] Inspector checks demo limits, new partition layout, fire-resistance ratings where required, door swings/egress, accessibility clearances, and that no structural members were altered without approval.

     Timing: After framing and layout are complete, before drywall or ceiling closure

  [Plumbing Rough-In] Inspector verifies grease interceptor location and sizing, floor drain placement and slope, restroom fixture rough-ins, venting, trap arms, cleanouts, and that the three-compartment sink is properly drained and vented.

     Timing: Before slab patching, insulation, or wall/ceiling closure

  [Electrical Rough-In] Inspector verifies panel upgrade installation, feeder sizing, breaker labeling, dedicated cooking equipment circuits, GFCI/AFCI where applicable, equipment disconnects, grounding/bonding, and working clearances at the panel.

     Timing: Before walls/ceilings are closed
