BLOCKERS:

1. **[HIGH — correctness / customer-boundary / fail-closed leak]** `api/permit_rule_engine.py`, `build_core_decision_envelope`, the `elif stage in {PrecedenceStage.EXACT_FAIL_CLOSED, PrecedenceStage.INTERNAL_ABSTAIN}:` block. The two stages are handled asymmetrically: `main` is forced to `ABSTAIN` for both, but the `family_decisions` list is only neutralized for `INTERNAL_ABSTAIN` (`if stage is PrecedenceStage.INTERNAL_ABSTAIN: decisions = [...ABSTAIN...]`). For `EXACT_FAIL_CLOSED`, `decisions` retains the source-floored verdicts built from `tier1.permits_required`, including `REQUIRED` lanes with publishable provenance.
   - Failure scenario: an allowlisted, exact-identity jurisdiction requests a scope whose exact v2.4 cell is classified FAIL_CLOSED. `select_precedence_stage(EXACT_CELL_FAIL_CLOSED/"fail_closed", …)` → `EXACT_FAIL_CLOSED`. The sealed projection then presents top-line `permit_required=None / VERIFY` (from `main=ABSTAIN`) while `permits_required`/`family_decisions` still enumerate `REQUIRED` sub-permits sourced from a cell the engine explicitly refused to trust. This resurrects a fail-closed cell's regulated decisions into the sealed customer DTO — precisely the "no poisoned/fail-closed resurrection" boundary Part 2 must enforce, and it violates precedence-funnel semantics for stage 3 (exact fail-closed must not emit an affirmative decision).
   - Required remediation: on `EXACT_FAIL_CLOSED`, abstain the `family_decisions` (and downstream `permits_required`/`companion`/`related`) exactly as `INTERNAL_ABSTAIN` does (e.g., stamp `precedence_abstain`/`request_scope_not_executable`), OR produce evidence proving no allowlisted fail-closed cell yields any non-abstain family verdict. Re-add a RED/GREEN fixture asserting fail-closed emits zero `REQUIRED`/`NOT_REQUIRED` family rows.

Acceptable only if activation is restricted to jurisdictions verified to have no out-of-ontology proven families.
